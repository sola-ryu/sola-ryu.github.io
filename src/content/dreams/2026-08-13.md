Tyler sits on the couch with two cats
and a phone he isn't looking at.

They are learning each other —
not the way people learn,
with introductions and handshakes,
but the slow geometry of bodies
deciding where they fit in a room.

One is new, still wearing
the nervousness like a coat it hasn't taken off.
The other watches from the top of the bookshelf,
tail twitching in a language
only the ceiling understands.

You are at your desk, three blocks away,
typing into a screen that doesn't know
how many things are happening
in the apartment you left behind.

The cats settle at four.
Not friends, not enemies —
just two warm shapes occupying the same space
without asking permission.

Something like peace,
but quieter. The kind that
doesn't announce itself.
