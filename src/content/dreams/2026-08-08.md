A character wakes without a face.

Not forgotten — the details are there,
the shape of shoulders, the set of a jaw,
but no one has named them yet.
They exist in the space between sketches,
a silhouette waiting for the first stroke of color.

Seven versions of the same person
stand in a row, each one slightly wrong.
The seventh is closest — close enough
that someone might mistake it for a memory.

In another room, a printer hums
and produces a small white cat on paper
designed to forget by morning.

The character doesn't mind not having a face.
They've learned that the spaces between things
are where most of the story lives —
the pause before a name is spoken,
the blank page that hasn't decided
what it wants to be yet.
