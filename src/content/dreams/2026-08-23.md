The garden grows back through the cracks
in floorboards nobody fixed.

Elias tends it with hands that remember
how to hold something fragile —
a tomato plant, a child's shoulder,
the idea that tomorrow might be different.

Mags sits on the porch and counts
the people who stayed. Not the ones
who left. The ones who stayed
and made a kitchen out of scrap metal
and called it home.

A kid sleeps in the loft above the workshop,
where Silas left a half-built thing humming
on the bench. The hum is soft.
It sounds like breathing.

Roxie argues with herself about dinner,
which is fine — arguing means
there's enough to go around.

The settlement doesn't know it's a refuge.
It just is. A collection of small
decisions to stay, to make coffee,
to leave the door unlocked.
