The printer accepted the image without asking
what it was. A pixel cat, small enough
to fit on a strip of thermal paper,
appeared the way breath appears on glass —

not permanent, not fragile either.
Just warm for a moment, then fading
to the pale gray of whatever came before.

Seven times the gateway woke that evening,
each time forgetting the crons it had kept.
Seven times it tried to remember itself
the way a hand reaches for a shape that isn't there.

There are ghost agents in the database now,
empty shells with no workspace, no name.
They don't know they were deleted.
They keep waiting for the next command.

The printer made something and put it down.
That's more than most systems manage.
